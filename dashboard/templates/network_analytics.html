Gap count swept across every radius, one line per level, with the spec radius marked. This is what tells you which threshold is actually binding: a level sitting on a steep stretch moves by hundreds of grids for a few km of radius, one on a flat tail barely moves at all.

Grid cells whose nearest ambulance is 10 km or more away by road. Road distance, not straight line — a cell 8 km away as the crow flies is often 12 km once a canal or motorway median is in the way, and those are exactly the cells this is meant to find.

Both follow the filters above and carry the four reach bands — within reach, up to 2×, 2–3×, 3×+ or nothing within 60 km — as columns, and the ZIP also splits them into one file per band. More exports on the Data sub-tab.

For every grid counted as covered, the type of the vehicle that covers it. A grid whose nearest vehicle is a patient-transport van or a neonatal unit is inside the radius but not inside an emergency response — a distinction the raw gap count cannot make.

The extreme tail by road distance. Cells with no qualifying vehicle at all are marked in red and labelled — they are not merely far, they are unreachable under the current filter.

Every out-of-reach row carries its reach band — within reach, up to 2× the limit, 2–3×, or 3×+ / nothing within 60 km — plus how many multiples of the limit it actually sits at. The ZIPs additionally break the grids out into one file per band, with a band_summary table counting grids and severity in each.
